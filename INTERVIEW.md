# Interview notes

**Buyer and sector.** The buyer is a municipal public works department in the government sector. Its intake and maintenance teams need reviewable reports, clear assignment, and a public account of progress. Residents need a simple reporting flow and understandable repair status.

**Resident workflow.** Browse photographic issues on a shared neighborhood map, upload a photo, confirm a precise location, add firsthand context, and review editable documentation assistance. Support an existing issue with one reversible action and follow planned, active, and completed work.

**Staff workflow.** Review the same report, correct the documentation, confirm it or preserve it as a linked duplicate, set independent priority with a reason, assign a team/date, and publish repair updates. Low-support access concerns can still be urgent. Public status and history remain attached to the intake record.

**Differentiation.** A basic form ends at submission. StreetFix connects discovery, community support, documentation, triage, and public repair history on the same map. AI assists documentation; staff retain urgency and assignment decisions. Missing-information prompts are more defensible than speculative hazards or safety scores.

**Proposed value.** Clearer intake, community visibility, and a connected repair workflow are hypotheses. The prototype has no measured adoption, cost, repair-time, or service-level outcomes. A role toggle and local identities demonstrate the workflow without pretending to offer municipal authorization or verified voting.

**Validation plan.** Run a small, consented pilot with residents and public works staff on representative reporting and triage tasks. First observe their existing intake process to establish a baseline. Compare the same task types, capture where participants need help, and interview them about status comprehension. Review draft factuality, accessibility, privacy, duplicate rules, and routing with staff before broader use.

| Proposed pilot metric | How to measure |
| --- | --- |
| Report completeness | Staff-defined rubric: usable photo, confirmed location, category, factual description, and needed contextual answers; compare first submitted reports with the baseline. |
| Time to prepare a reviewable report | Time from beginning intake until a staff reviewer judges the report reviewable; include follow-up/corrections and do not count speculative AI text as completeness. |
| Staff triage time | Time to review, resolve a duplicate, choose priority, and assign a team on comparable cases; record decision quality as well as speed. |
| Resident understanding of repair status | Ask residents to identify the current stage, responsible team, and meaning of any target date after viewing a report; use short comprehension interviews. |

Begin with one neighborhood and a manageable public works team. Agree on success criteria before the pilot, collect both quantitative tasks and qualitative feedback, and report observed results with their sample size and limitations. No improvement percentages are assumed.
